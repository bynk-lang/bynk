# 1029 — A Bearer or Oidc arm of an actor sum reads its verified claims through a second pattern binder

- **Status:** Accepted (v0.766)

**Context.** #3937 gave the binder of a lone `Bearer` or `Oidc` actor its verified claims as `u.claims`. A `Bearer` or `Oidc` arm of an actor sum (`by who: User | Visitor`) could not read them: the sum's binder is the resolved arm, and the arm `User(u)` binds `u` to the identity value itself, so `u.claims` had no receiver (#4344). An `Oidc` actor may be a member of a sum since #3936, so both scheme's arms are affected.

**Decision.** A: a `Bearer` or `Oidc` arm of an `http` handler's sum may bind a second name, `User(u, c)`, with `c: TokenClaims`. The one-name `User(u)` is unchanged, and an arm that writes it reads no claims. B: only a `Bearer` or `Oidc` arm has the second binder. A second binder on any other arm (`Visitor`, `Signature`, `SignedCookie`), on an arm of a non-`http` sum, or a third on a `Bearer` or `Oidc` arm is `bynk.types.pattern_arity`, with a note saying what an arm binds. C: the claims come only from the token the seam verified. The generated Worker builds the arm's `who` as `{ tag, identity, claims }` from the verified token's claims after the signature, expiry and identity checks, and a request that fails them never resolves the arm, so a forged or unsigned token reads no claims and an open `Visitor` arm has none. D: a test supplies the claims as the principal's second argument, as for a lone actor: `by User("ada", TokenClaims.of([…]))` at `unit`, `integration` and `system`; with none given the arm reads none. At `system` the driver signs them into the token it sends, as a `claims` object for an `Oidc` arm and beside `sub` for a `Bearer` one. E: the two options #4344 left open are decided here. Option 1 (a second pattern binder) is chosen. Option 2 (`who.claims` on the sum binder, valid only inside a `Bearer` or `Oidc` arm) is rejected: it needs the sum binder to be flow-typed by the arm it is in, a mechanism the checker does not have, and it reads as valid anywhere the binder is in scope while being an error on every other arm, where a pattern binder simply does not exist. The second binder reuses the claims slot the call site already has, so a handler and its test read the same way.

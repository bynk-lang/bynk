# 1026 — A rate-limited route's 429 is part of its wire contract

- **Status:** Accepted (v0.762)

**Context.** A route with `@rateLimit(Limiter, key: …)` can answer `429` before its body is read, with a `Retry-After` header (#3926, #4407, #4422). The wire contract that `bynk openapi` and the generated client both read listed no `429` for it, so a client generated from the spec had no typed way to handle the limit, and a caller could not tell that the route could refuse them.

**Decision.** The shared wire contract (`http_responses`) adds `429 RateLimited` as a boundary-implicit answer to every handler carrying `@rateLimit`, like the router's own `400`, `413` and `415`: one change feeds both consumers, so they cannot drift. `bynk openapi` documents it with a required `Retry-After` header (an integer number of seconds), as the router's `{ kind, details }` body, or as `application/problem+json` under `errors { format: problem }`, mirroring how the same service's `413` and `415` are written. The generated client types it as a `RateLimited` member of the route's response union, unconditionally, decoded by the same body kind as the other router refusals. A route without `@rateLimit` lists and generates nothing new.

**Consequences.** The generated client's union for a limited route is a closed sum that grows a member, so an exhaustive `switch` on such a route stops type-checking until it handles `RateLimited`, which is the point: a route that can answer `429` and does not say so is the bug. The contract hash of a route moves when `@rateLimit` is added or removed, and for no other route. The retry delay is the `Retry-After` header, documented in the OpenAPI document; the client member carries the body, whose `details` names the seconds, and does not yet surface the header as a field.

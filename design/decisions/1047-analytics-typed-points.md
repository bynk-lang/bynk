# 1047 — A record type is an Analytics data point's schema

- **Status:** Accepted (v0.785)

**Context.** A `DataPoint` is `{ index, blobs, doubles }`, so its meaning is positional: SQL's `blob1` is whatever the writer put first, writers and readers agree by hand, and reordering a write silently changes what every later report reads (#4105).

**Decision.** A record type `T` is the schema, and the compiler owns the mapping from it, as it owns a column list for D1's `select[T]`. Delivered in three changes. (1, this one) `Cap.writeValue(v)` / `Cap.writeValue[T](v)` on a derived `Analytics` instance: text-like fields (`String` and refined or opaque ones, payload-less enums by variant name, `Bool` as `"true"`/`"false"`) are `blob1…` and numeric fields (`Int`, `Float`, `Decimal` as its float value, `Duration` and `Instant` as milliseconds) are `double1…`, each in declaration order; at most one `@index` `String` field is `index1`. More than 20 of either, a second `@index`, a non-`String` `@index` or any other field type is `bynk.types.analytics_point_shape`, naming the field. Like `select[T]`, `writeValue` is not a declared operation: the checker records `Callee::AnalyticsWrite` with the mapping and the emitter lowers it over `write(DataPoint)`, so the platform limits, stubs and observation are `write`'s. `write` stays for points that are not a record. (2) `AnalyticsSql.select[T](Instance, "FROM {dataset} …", params)` writes the `SELECT` list from the mapping, checks the read type's fields against the instance's one written type or tail aliases, and decodes. (3) The lock records each instance's mapping in `bynk.schema.lock`; reordering, retyping or removing a field is `bynk.analytics.mapping_changed` until accepted, and appending is allowed. The accept follows the existing #3956 accept-order pattern (the `ord_order_changed` diagnostic and its accept) folded into today's flow, not a new `bynk lock` command family.

**Consequences.** Old points keep their old positions, which is why (3) exists; until it lands, reordering a written record is the author's to avoid, and the reference says so. A `Decimal` written as a double loses exactness; the platform stores doubles. `@index` is accepted on any record field and means something only to `writeValue`.

## ADR: bad-principal-sole-refuser
title: A bare bad credential on an actor sum reaches its one refusing member, whatever the scheme
summary: the checker resolves a bare `by <Bad>` whose one refusing member is an `Oidc` or `SignedCookie` seam to that member, and the case drives it as if the member were named

**Context.** Part A of #4385 let a bad-credential principal name the sum member it tests (`by Forged(Browser)`) and made a bare principal that two or more members could refuse an error. It kept the old bare path for a sole `Bearer` or `Signature` refuser, which has its own driver. A sole `Oidc` or `SignedCookie` refuser (`by Expired` on `Browser | Hook`, where only the cookie refuses an expired credential) had no such driver, so the bare form was refused with `expired_needs_bearer_route` even though `by Expired(Browser)` worked.

**Decision.** A: the sum check resolves a bare principal that exactly one member can refuse and whose seam is `Oidc` or `SignedCookie`, and records that member on the clause as `CallSitePrincipal::BadCredentialArm`, the same recorded fact the emitter already reads for every principal; the emitter drives it through the `sumbad` driver exactly as if the member were written, so there is no second driver path and the golden for a bare and a named call are the same. B: a sole `Bearer` or `Signature` refuser keeps its old driver, so nothing that compiled before drives differently. C: this only relaxes a refusal (the bare forms it admits were errors), so it breaks no program. D: `by Revoked(Arm)` on a `sessions = true` cookie member was added in part A and is exercised there; here the bare `by Revoked` reaches the one cookie member that can refuse it.

## ADR: queue-admission-order
title: A signed queue consumer verifies a message, checks it is fresh, leases its id while the handler runs, and acknowledges anything refused
summary: The admission step verifies the signature, refuses a message signed longer ago than the replay window or too far ahead, and claims the message id on a short lease that the entry confirms once the message is handled and releases if the handler asks for a retry or throws, so unsigned traffic cannot fill the replay store and a failed message is redelivered rather than lost; every refusal is acknowledged and reported rather than handled, retried or dead-lettered

**Context.** Part of #3899 (Decisions F and G, at the library level). A consumer that admits only signed messages must refuse the others without a retry that could never succeed, must recognise a message that was put back on the queue, and must not lose a message whose handler failed for a transient reason.

**Decision.** `queueAdmit(received, consumer, sourceQueue, ports)` verifies under the SOURCE queue's name, so a dead-letter consumer verifies a message the platform moved. It then refuses a message whose `ts` is more than the 15-day replay window in the past, or more than 5 minutes ahead of the consumer's clock, as `stale`: a claim made at most one window after `ts` and held for one window can only lapse once the message is already older than the window, so every replay is either claimed or stale. The id is claimed per consumer and source queue on a 15-minute lease, and the admission carries `settle(handled)`: the entry confirms the claim for the full window once the message is handled, and releases it when the handler asks for a retry or throws, so the platform's redelivery of a failed message is admitted again. A claim whose instance dies lapses with its lease for the same reason. The consumer's admitted senders and keys, and the claim, confirm and release, are ports the entry supplies, so the step carries no storage or deploy detail. A refusal (unsigned, malformed, version, unknown sender, bad signature, stale, replayed) has its own fault text naming the consumer.

**Consequences.** The runtime step and its tests land with no emitter or checker change: no program changes meaning, and the refusal of `Producer` refinements and `Foreign` actors on queue consumers stays. Wiring the step into the generated `queue()` entry needs the consumer's keys and the Worker's id-claim binding, which come with the consumer slice, so it lands there. Hover on a `Producer` refinement is the same new surface as the `Sender` one (#4393) and is left with it.

## ADR: queue-admission-order
title: A signed queue consumer verifies a message, then claims its id, and acknowledges anything refused
summary: The admission step verifies the signature first and claims the message id only for a verified message, so unsigned traffic cannot fill the replay store, and every refusal, a replay included, is acknowledged and reported rather than handled, retried or dead-lettered

**Context.** Part of #3899 (Decisions F and G, at the library level). A consumer that admits only signed messages must refuse the others without a retry that could never succeed, and must recognise a message that was put back on the queue.

**Decision.** `queueAdmit(received, consumer, sourceQueue, ports)` verifies under the SOURCE queue's name, so a dead-letter consumer verifies a message the platform moved, then claims the id per consumer and source queue for the replay window. The consumer's admitted senders and their keys, and the id claim, are ports the entry supplies, so the step carries no storage or deploy detail. A refusal (unsigned, malformed, version, unknown sender, bad signature, replayed) has its own fault text naming the consumer.

**Consequences.** The runtime step and its tests land with no emitter or checker change: no program changes meaning, and the refusal of `Producer` refinements and `Foreign` actors on queue consumers stays. Wiring the step into the generated `queue()` entry needs the consumer's keys and the Worker's id-claim binding, which come with producer signing and deploy keys in the next slice, so it lands there. Hover on a `Producer` refinement is the same new surface as the `Sender` one (#4393) and is left with it.

## ADR: store-scans-stop-early
title: Which store scans stop reading pages early
summary: any/all over Map and Log, and first/firstOrElse/take(n).collect over Log, read a page at a time and stop at the answer

**Context.** A store query over a `Map` or `Log` read every entry in pages and only then ran its terminal, so `log.first()` or `map.any(p)` paid for the whole collection even when the first page held the answer. A `Map`'s scan order is its insertion order (ADR 0109), but its storage keys are in key order, so the first page of a `Map` read is not its first-inserted entries. A `Log`'s keys are its append times, so its pages are its order.

**Decision.** The terminals `any` and `all` over a `Map` or `Log`, and `first`, `firstOrElse` and `take(n).collect()` over a `Log` only, read the collection a page at a time (`lazyValues()` on the store handle, the runtime's `scan` stages) and stop at the answer. Stages ahead of the terminal are `filter`, `map` and `flatMap`, and on a `Log` `skip`; the staged writes of the handler are merged into each page as for every other read. `first`, `firstOrElse` and `take` over a `Map` read everything as before, because their answer depends on insertion order, which only a whole read recovers. An equality `filter` on an `@indexed` field keeps its posting-list route. Every other shape, and a query held in a `let`, keeps the array path.

Rejected: (B) store each `Map` entry's insertion sequence in its key so that pages are in insertion order. That changes the stored key layout of every existing `Map`, needs a migration, and makes a keyed `get` need a second lookup. (C) read a `Map` in key order and sort the pages already read, stopping when no unread key can precede them. Insertion sequences are not bounded by key order, so no page boundary ever proves that, and it reads everything for the cost of a more complex scan.

**Consequences.** The results of every query are unchanged; only the pages read differ. A `Map` that is `any`/`all`-scanned is read in key order, which is unobservable since the answer does not depend on order. Hover on the terminal of a `Map` or `Log` pipeline (`any`, `all`, `first`, `firstOrElse`, `take(n).collect()`) adds a line saying whether it stops at its answer or reads every entry, and why. Completion, semantic tokens and signature help are unchanged, because no declaration, type or syntax changed.
